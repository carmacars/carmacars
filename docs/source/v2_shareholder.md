## Parties

This Agreement is made as of the Effective Date among Holdco A, Holdco B, Abbas Al-Mashita ("Abbas"), Mahad Hussain ("Mahad") and 18122211 Canada Inc. (the "Corporation"), which operates as CarmaCars and is the franchisee under the Franchise Agreement.

## Article 2 – The Corporation

**2.1 Status.** This is a unanimous shareholder agreement under section 146 of the Act among all holders of Shares. It restricts the directors' powers, and to that extent the Shareholders have the directors' rights, duties and liabilities. It binds the Corporation, prevails over its articles and by-laws, and permits nothing the Franchise Agreement prohibits. Each Side shall vote and act to carry it out.

**2.2 Business.** The Corporation shall carry on only the Business, for the equal benefit of both Sides.

**2.3 Holding corporations.** If anyone else holds Shares on the Effective Date, the Principals shall cause Holdco A and Holdco B each to hold 50% by the Holdco Date, after notice to the Franchisor.

**2.4 Legends.** Every Share certificate or register entry shall carry the Schedule D legends. No one becomes a holder of Shares without first agreeing in writing to be bound as a Shareholder, with whoever controls it bound as a Principal.

**2.5 Merger.** Each Side shall bring its existing car rental business into the Corporation by the Contribution Date: the businesses, vehicles, listings and accounts in Schedule E. A contributed vehicle that a Side owns is a Shareholder Vehicle. Everything else contributed passes to the Corporation for no payment. Revenue from bookings starting on or after the Contribution Date is the Corporation's. Each Side keeps its business's earlier liabilities.

## Article 3 – Capital and Funding

**3.1 Ownership.** Each Shareholder owns 50% of the Shares, unless both Sides agree otherwise in writing.

**3.2 Initial Investment.** Each Side shall invest the Initial Investment by the Investment Date, in the form the Accountant recommends. A Side's loan to the Corporation is a "Shareholder Loan".

**3.3 Later funding.** Later funding by the Sides is made only under a call with Joint Approval (a "Funding Call"), equally, at the same time and on the same terms. Neither Side may otherwise fund the Corporation. A payment one Side makes for it with Joint Approval is a Shareholder Loan until matched or repaid.

**3.4 Funding default.** If a Side does not fund its share of the Initial Investment or a Funding Call when due, and the other Side has:

- it shall pay within 10 business days after notice;
- failing that, the other Side may withdraw its advance or lend the shortfall (a "Shortfall Loan") at the Default Rate, repaid before any Distribution or other Shareholder Loan;
- meanwhile, Distributions and loan repayments payable to the defaulting Side go against the Shortfall Loan; and
- no Shares are issued and neither Side is diluted.

**3.5 Shareholder Loans.** Unless Joint Approval sets other terms, any other Shareholder Loan bears no interest, is unsecured, is repaid only with Joint Approval and equally to both Sides, and ranks behind arm's length creditors.

## Article 4 – Distributions

**4.1 Always equal.** Withdrawals and Distributions need Joint Approval of amount and timing and are made equally, in the same form, to both Sides at the same time.

**4.2 Unequal payments.** A Side that receives more than the other holds the excess in trust for the Corporation and shall repay it within 10 business days after demand, with interest at the Default Rate. Meanwhile the Corporation shall withhold the same amount from that Side.

## Article 5 – Revenue and Expenses

**5.1 Expenses.** The Corporation pays the Franchise Fee and its HST (reimbursing or crediting any part a Side has paid), the fixed annual Insurance Program premium, directly to the insurer or broker, and every other cost of the Business, except costs an owner bears under Section 6.3 or its owner agreement.

**5.2 Revenue.** All revenue from every vehicle is paid to the Corporation. It pays each Third-Party Owner the share in its owner agreement and keeps the rest, including that owner's insurance, tracker and other fees. It keeps all Corporation-Only Revenue on every vehicle, including Shareholder Vehicles.

**5.3 Referrals.** Owners and in-person staff, but not virtual assistants, earn the Kickback Rate on the rental charge of a referred renter's first rental only (a "Referral Kickback"), deducted before Owner Revenue is calculated. None is earned on mileage overage fees, Corporation-Only Revenue or taxes.

**5.4 Renter damage.** Damage below the Damage Threshold is billed to the renter at actual cost. Larger damage goes through an insurance claim, with the renter billed a deductible equal to the Damage Threshold.

## Article 6 – Shareholder Vehicles

**6.1 Owner's car.** A Shareholder Vehicle is its Vehicle Owner's car, not the Corporation's, even if registered to the Corporation. Each is entered in the Schedule C register before its first booking. Neither Side must contribute one, or needs the other's consent to add one that meets the Corporation's fleet standards and the insurer's requirements.

**6.2 Economics.**

| Item | Vehicle Owner | Corporation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner Revenue | Owner Share | Corporation Share |
| Claim Profit, per claim, once all money is in and costs are known | Owner Share | Corporation Share |
| Claim shortfall | Owner Share | Corporation Share |
| Corporation-Only Revenue | None | All |
| Insurance and tracker fees | Pays none | Charges none |

**6.3 Costs.** The Vehicle Owner pays the costs of owning its vehicle, including financing, registration, maintenance and repairs not paid by a claim, and indemnifies the Corporation for anything it must pay the vehicle's lender. The Corporation pays the insurance premium and supplies the tracker.

**6.4 Bare trust.** If a Shareholder Vehicle is registered to the Corporation, the Corporation holds title as bare trustee for the Vehicle Owner, shall sign a bare trust declaration, shall not sell or encumber the vehicle, and shall deal with title only as the Vehicle Owner directs.

**6.5 Return.** A Vehicle Owner may withdraw a vehicle on 30 days' notice, subject to confirmed bookings. When a Side stops being a Shareholder, or the Corporation is wound up, its Shareholder Vehicles go back to it: the Corporation shall transfer title as directed within 10 business days after request, at the Vehicle Owner's cost.

## Article 7 – Governance

**7.1 Board.** The Corporation has two directors, one nominated by each Side, initially Abbas and Mahad. Only the nominating Side may remove or replace its director.

**7.2 Voting.** Each director has one vote, and no chair has a casting vote. A directors' resolution needs both votes. Quorum is both directors or both Shareholders.

**7.3 Joint Approval.** The Corporation shall not do anything listed in Schedule B without Joint Approval.

**7.4 Signing.** Either Principal may alone sign routine documents and approve ordinary-course payments up to the Signing Limit. Anything else, and any payment to a Side or its Affiliate except a scheduled Owner Payout, needs both.

**7.5 Banking.** All revenue is deposited to the Corporation's own accounts, which no Principal may use for cash withdrawals or personal expenses. Both Principals have full access to every account, platform and system of the Corporation, which owns the credentials.

**7.6 Protected payments.** Despite any Deadlock, either Principal may alone cause the Corporation to make these when due, and neither shall block them: insurance premiums; the Royalty and monthly fleet report; rent; approved payroll; taxes; Owner Payouts; and renter refunds.

**7.7 Work.** The workload is shared equally. Each Principal shall give the Business the time it reasonably needs and act in good faith. No Principal, Affiliate or family member is paid for work without Joint Approval, and approved pay is equal for both Sides. Receipted Business expenses are reimbursed.

**7.8 Records.** Each Side and its advisers may inspect and copy all books, records and data of the Corporation at any reasonable time, and for two years after it stops being a Shareholder for records of its time as one.

**7.9 Property.** All brands, domains, software, data and accounts created for or used in the Business belong to the Corporation, and each Principal shall transfer them to it.

## Article 8 – Franchise and Guarantees

**8.1 Shared compliance.** Each party has read the Franchise Agreement. Compliance with it, on which the Insurance Program depends, is a shared obligation: the Sides shall together cause the Corporation to perform the obligations in Schedule D, and each shall promptly tell the other of any notice from the Franchisor, insurer or broker. No vehicle is rented while uninsured.

**8.2 Represented Persons.** The Represented Persons are insured parties on the insurance policy and Guarantors. They are not Shareholders, directors or officers and have no right to Shares, votes, Distributions or management. Each Principal is the policy representative of his Represented Person: he is responsible for, and speaks for, that person in all dealings with the Corporation, insurer, broker and Franchisor, who, with the other Side, may rely on what he says or signs. He shall cause that person to sign what the insurer, broker or Franchisor reasonably requires, keep his or her Guarantee in force and acknowledge this Section in writing. His Side is liable for any loss that person causes.

**8.3 Sharing the Guarantees.** Between the Sides:

- the Corporation shall indemnify each Guarantor for anything paid under a Guarantee;
- failing that, each Side bears half of the total paid by all Guarantors, including enforcement costs, whichever Guarantor is pursued, and may recover any excess from the other; but
- a Side bears the whole of a claim caused by its breach of this Agreement, fraud or gross negligence, or by its Represented Person.

**8.4 Exit.** When a Side stops being a Shareholder, the other Side and the Corporation shall: use all reasonable efforts to obtain the Franchisor's release of its Guarantees on closing, the departing Side signing any covenant the Franchisor may require for it; failing a release, indemnify the departing Side and its Guarantors for Guarantee liability from later events; and have its Represented Person removed as an insured party at the next renewal or earlier.

## Article 9 – Share Transfers

**9.1 Restriction.** No Shareholder may Transfer Shares, and no Principal may Transfer or issue shares of his Shareholder or allow its Change of Control, except under Articles 9 to 11. A Transfer in breach is void. Shares of a Shareholder may go to its Principal's spouse, children or family trust, without a Change of Control, on 15 business days' notice.

**9.2 Franchisor's rights.** Every Transfer and Share issue is subject to these.

| Transfer or issue | Franchisor is entitled to |
| --- | --- |
| Among the Guarantors, their spouses and children, or to a trust or corporation Guarantors control, if the Guarantors together keep control of the Corporation and their Guarantees stay in force | Written notice |
| Any other | Prior written consent; a right of first refusal ranking ahead of Section 9.3; and the Transfer Fee |

Nothing closes until the notice is given, or the consent is obtained and the Franchisor's right of first refusal has ended. The Side whose Transfer triggers the Transfer Fee and the Franchisor's costs, or its buyer, pays them.

**9.3 First refusal.** A Shareholder (the "Seller") may sell to an arm's length buyer only under a written cash offer for all of its Shares and Shareholder Loans, naming the buyer and who controls it. It shall deliver the offer to the other Shareholder and to the Corporation, which shall at once deliver it to the Franchisor. The other Shareholder may buy on the offer's terms by notice within 30 days after receiving it or, if later, 10 business days after the Franchisor's right of first refusal ends. If neither buys, the Seller may sell to the named buyer on those terms within 60 days after the Franchisor approves; otherwise this Section applies again.

**9.4 Conditions.** No Transfer closes unless Section 9.2 is complied with, the transferee is bound under Section 2.4, everything the transferor's Side owes the Corporation or the other Side is paid, and the Corporation is not put in default under the Franchise Agreement or an insurance policy.

## Article 10 – Deadlock and Shotgun

**10.1 Deadlock Notice.** Either Side may give the other written notice describing a Deadlock (a "Deadlock Notice"). The Principals shall meet in person and try in good faith to resolve it within 20 business days after the Deadlock Notice. Meanwhile the matter does not proceed, the Business carries on as usual, and neither Side shall harm it or pressure the other by withholding work, access or money.

**10.2 Mediation.** If the Deadlock is not resolved within those 20 business days, either Side may by written notice refer it to mediation in Toronto before one mediator chosen by the Sides or, failing agreement, appointed by the ADR Institute of Ontario at either Side's request. The Sides shall take part in good faith and share the mediator's costs equally. The mediation is confidential and without prejudice. A Side that does not cooperate in appointing the mediator or attend a scheduled session has failed to take part.

**10.3 Shotgun Offer.** If a Deadlock is still unresolved 60 days after the Deadlock Notice, and the mediation under Section 10.2 has been held or the other Side has failed to take part in it, either Shareholder (the "Offeror") may give the other (the "Recipient") a written offer (a "Shotgun Offer") that:

- states one cash price per Share;
- offers both to buy all of the Recipient's Shares and to sell all of the Offeror's Shares at that price, the buyer also buying the seller's Shareholder Loans at face amount;
- is irrevocable for 45 days and unconditional, except for any Franchisor notice or consent; and
- comes with the Deposit, paid in trust to the Corporation's lawyer.

**10.4 Election.** Within those 45 days the Recipient shall elect in writing to sell or to buy at that price. No election is an election to sell. The election is a binding agreement for cash. A Recipient electing to buy shall pay the Deposit within 5 business days, and the Offeror's is returned. A buyer that fails to close forfeits its Deposit.

**10.5 Limits.** No Shotgun Offer may be given: before the Shotgun Start Date; while an Article 11 purchase is in progress; by a Side with an uncured Triggering Event under paragraph (c) or (d); or to a Side within 180 days after its Principal dies or becomes unable to work.

**10.6 Franchisor.** A purchase between the Shareholders is intended to need only notice to the Franchisor, with the Guarantees staying in force. If consent is required, the buyer obtains it and pays any Transfer Fee.

**10.7 No winding-up.** Neither Side shall apply to wind up the Corporation over a Deadlock unless this Article has been completed without a closing.

## Article 11 – Forced Buyout

**11.1 Option.** The Buying Side may buy all of the Affected Side's Shares and Shareholder Loans by notice within 180 days after learning of a death or Disability, or 90 days after learning of any other Triggering Event or after a disputed Material Breach is decided. Otherwise the Affected Side, or its Principal's estate, continues as a Shareholder.

**11.2 Price.** The price is the Buyout Percentage of the Shares' Fair Market Value at the end of the month before the Triggering Event, plus the outstanding Shareholder Loans.

**11.3 Payment.** The buyer pays the Down Payment on closing and the balance in equal monthly instalments over the Instalment Term at the Instalment Rate, prepayable and secured by a pledge of the purchased Shares. The balance falls due if an instalment is over 15 days late or the buyer sells the Business.

**11.4 Valuation.** If the Sides do not agree on Fair Market Value within 20 business days after the option notice, an independent Chartered Business Valuator, chosen by the Sides or failing agreement by the Accountant, determines it as an expert within 45 days. The determination is final, absent obvious error. The Corporation pays the fee, or the Affected Side on a Material Breach.

**11.5 Closing.** Every purchase between the Sides closes within 60 days after the notice, election or valuation that fixes it, or up to 60 days later if a Franchisor consent is required. On closing:

- the seller delivers its Shares free of encumbrances and assigns its Shareholder Loans;
- its director and officers resign, and its Side hands over all property, records and credentials of the Corporation and releases the Corporation from all claims except amounts owing under this Agreement; and
- the buyer pays the price due, less anything the seller's Side owes the buyer's Side or the Corporation.

**11.6 Seller default.** If the seller does not close, the buyer may pay the price to the Corporation's lawyer in trust. The sale is then complete, and the seller irrevocably appoints the buyer's nominee director as its attorney to sign the transfer, a power that survives death or incapacity.

## Article 12 – Restrictive Covenants

**12.1 Non-competition.** During the Restricted Period, no Shareholder or Principal shall, anywhere in the Territory, directly or indirectly carry on, own an interest in, finance, advise, work for, or act as a director, officer, partner or agent of, a Restricted Business, other than through the Corporation.

**12.2 No carve-out.** There is no exception for any vehicle rental business of either Side or its Affiliates, because both Sides' businesses are merging into the Corporation under Section 2.5. Any such business in the Territory is carried on through the Corporation or not at all, unless the other Side consents in writing. Operating an existing business only as needed to complete Section 2.5 is not a breach.

**12.3 Non-solicitation.** During the Restricted Period, no Shareholder or Principal shall, other than for the Corporation: solicit for a Restricted Business anyone who was the Corporation's renter or customer in the preceding 24 months; solicit a Third-Party Owner or investor to remove a vehicle or reduce its dealings with the Corporation; or solicit or hire any of the Corporation's employees, virtual assistants or contractors.

**12.4 Confidentiality.** Each party shall keep confidential, and use only for the Business, all non-public information about the Corporation, the Business, the other Side, the Franchise Agreement and the Insurance Program, without time limit, except as the law requires.

**12.5 Enforcement.** These covenants add to any given to the Franchisor, whose permission for Guarantors to own other vehicle rental businesses does not limit them. They are given by business owners, not employees, and each covenant, and each part of the Territory and Restricted Period, is severable. The Corporation and the other Side are entitled to an injunction, and either Side may enforce this Article for the Corporation alone.

## Article 13 – General

**13.1 Principals.** Abbas and Mahad each sign personally and are bound by every provision that refers to a Principal or a Side. Each Principal guarantees his Shareholder's performance of this Agreement as primary obligor, and shall keep his own Guarantee in force.

**13.2 Term.** This Agreement runs from the Effective Date until one person holds all of the Shares, the Corporation is dissolved, or both Sides end it in writing. Articles 11 and 12, this Article and Sections 6.5, 7.8, 8.3 and 8.4 survive and bind each Principal's estate.

**13.3 Notices.** Notices are in writing, by hand, courier or email to the address in Schedule A, and are received on delivery or, for an email sent after 5:00 p.m. or on a non-business day, the next business day. A Deadlock Notice, a Shotgun Offer and an Article 11 notice must also go by hand or courier.

**13.4 General.** Ontario law governs, and the Ontario courts in Toronto have jurisdiction. This is the entire agreement on its subject. Amendments and waivers must be in writing signed by both Shareholders and both Principals, but both Principals may set Schedule A values in a signed document. An invalid provision does not affect the rest. This Agreement binds heirs, successors and permitted assigns, and may be signed in counterparts and electronically.

**13.5 Advice.** Each party was told to obtain independent legal and tax advice on this jointly prepared Agreement, had the chance to, and signs voluntarily.
